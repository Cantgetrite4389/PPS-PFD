Key differences vs. generic Macallum PFD — Fortron-specific features:
• NaSH + NaOH sulfide source (not Na₂S·9H₂O): Hoechst Celanese practice uses aqueous NaSH + NaOH to generate Na₂S in-situ in NMP (MV-01). Avoids crystallisation-water handling issues and gives better ionic purity control.
• NaOAc polymerisation aid (T-02): Sodium acetate added at ~0.05–0.2 mol/mol S. Promotes granular, higher-MW product and suppresses reactor wall scale — critical for commercial batch operation.
• Two-stage reactor sequence (R-01 → R-02): Stage 1 at 210–230°C drives prepolymer formation to ~80–95% conversion. Stage 2 at 250–270°C with deliberate water injection (6–15 mol/kg NMP) creates a two-phase system that drives chain-growth to high-MW linear PPS. This is the defining feature of the Hoechst/Ticona process versus Phillips (Ryton) single-stage + curing route.
• No curing oven: Fortron grades are linear, high-MW PPS. MW is built entirely in-reactor. No post-polymerisation air oxidation/cross-linking is applied — unlike Ryton P-grade and similar. This gives film/fibre processability and narrow MWD.
• VLE-controlled quench (QV-01): Water addition to precipitate PPS from solution is calculated from vapour-liquid equilibrium models (not empirical), giving tight control of particle size distribution — critical for filtration, washing efficiency, and powder flowability.
• Titanium / Hastelloy C-276 throughout reactors: Required for HCl/NaCl service at temperature. Wall-scale management via high-shear agitator geometry is a key engineering challenge noted in the patent literature.
• N₂ drying (not air): Fortron linear grades are dried under N₂ to prevent premature oxidation of chain ends. Contrast with older cured grades dried/cured in air.
• NMP impurity profile (DE 19614126A1): Fortron-specific GC/MS data show thiophenol (~30 ppm), dihydrofuranone (~10 ppm), tetrahydrothiophenone (~10 ppm) as characteristic NMP decomposition by-products — useful for process forensics and ETP design.
